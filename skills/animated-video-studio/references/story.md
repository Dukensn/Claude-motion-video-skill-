# Story craft — how to invent a story worth 4 minutes of someone's attention

The engine can draw anything simple; what makes a video work is the story. A demo that "shows the engine" is not a
story. Every video — comedy, What if, history, moral, fight — needs a **person who wants something**, a **reason it's
hard**, and a **change** by the end. Do this work before anything else, and do it seriously.

## 1. Three concepts, not one

Write **three radically different concepts** for the student's kind of video (different hero, world, tone, ending).
Each concept is 4 lines:

- **Title** (2–4 words, intriguing).
- **Logline**: *When [inciting event], [specific character with a flaw] must [goal] before [stakes / clock], but [obstacle].*
- **The hook image**: the first 3 seconds, what we see.
- **The turn**: the one thing the viewer won't see coming.

Then **score each concept honestly** (1–5) and keep only one above 18/25:

| | question |
| --- | --- |
| Clarity | Could a 12-year-old retell it after one viewing? |
| Desire | Does the hero want something concrete we can *see* (not "be happy")? |
| Surprise | Is there a turn that recontextualises what came before? |
| Emotion | Will the viewer feel something specific (laugh, ache, awe, dread)? |
| Freshness | Have we seen this exact idea a hundred times? (5 = never) |

Rewrite or replace a weak concept instead of polishing it. Offer the best one (or the top two) to the student.

## 2. Build the story with "but" and "therefore"

List the beats. Between every two beats write **BUT** or **THEREFORE** — never "and then". If a beat only connects
with "and then", it's filler: cut it or make it cause the next one.

> The baker saves the last croissant for his daughter's birthday — BUT a pigeon steals it — THEREFORE he chases it
> across the city — BUT the chase wrecks a wedding — THEREFORE the bride… — BUT the pigeon was feeding its own chicks —
> THEREFORE he…

## 3. Characters people remember

- **One want, one flaw, one detail.** Want = what they chase. Flaw = why it's hard from the inside (pride, fear,
  impatience). Detail = the thing we'd recognise in a silhouette (a too-big coat, a broken umbrella, a whistle).
- Show who they are in the first 5 seconds through an action, not a caption.
- The antagonist (a person, an animal, an object, a habit, nature) wants something too — that makes it interesting.
- The hero **changes** or **proves something** by the end. If nothing changed, it was an anecdote.

## 4. Specific beats generic

"A man is sad" → nothing. "A man sets two plates every night, then puts one back" → a story. Replace every generic
beat with a concrete, visual, slightly surprising image. Prefer one strange true detail over three adjectives.

## 5. Structures that work (pick one, don't copy films)

- **The escalating plan** (comedy, action): simple goal → plan → it goes wrong → bigger plan → worse → twist → button.
- **The countdown** (What if, thriller): a clock appears in the hook; each section is a step closer.
- **The mirror** (moral, emotional): the opening image returns at the end, changed (two plates → two plates, both used).
- **The investigation** (documentary, history): a mystery in the hook, clues, a reveal that reframes the beginning.
- **The underdog** (history, sport): the odds as numbers on screen, three failures, the turning idea, the win (or the noble loss).
- **The reversal** (any): the viewer believes A for 80 % of the film; the last 20 % shows it was B — and every earlier
  beat still makes sense.

## 6. Tone is a choice

Mix on purpose: comedy that lands one tender beat; a sad story with one laugh; a documentary with one absurd detail.
Decide where the counter-tone appears (usually the last third).

## 7. Sound is part of the story — music is optional

Decide scene by scene: **music**, **only ambience and effects**, or **silence**. Many great shorts have no music for
long stretches; silence before a twist, a single sound effect for a punchline, room tone for loneliness. Music should
start, stop and change on purpose, never wallpaper. `score.py` can be empty for a section, or for the whole film.

## 8. The table read (before you build)

Read the script as a stranger, out loud in your head, at real speed. Mark: where would I scroll away? where am I
confused? where did I smile? Fix every "scroll away" and every "confused". Only then show it to the student.
