# Type: Documentary

"How did ancient humans survive the deadliest winters?" A narrated, researched stickman documentary: curious, visual, never a textbook. 3, 8, 12 or 20 minutes.

## Stage 1 — ideas (then stop)

Ask the niche if they haven't given one (Ancient humans, Weird history, Science, Psychology, Animals, Space, Survival, Technology, Civilizations, Dark history, Haiti and the Caribbean…). Roll `python3 SKILL/scripts/ideas.py documentary`. Write **10 original titles** + one sentence each on the central question. Titles create curiosity with Why / How / What / When or "The real reason…", are understandable without expertise, and promise a journey. Do not reuse well-known YouTube titles. End with "Choose a number (1–10)." and wait.

## Stage 2 — duration (then stop)

3 min (short, fast) · 8 min (more context) · 12 min (several sections) · 20 min (long-form, evidence and context). Ask and wait.

## Stage 3 — the script

Default arc (adapt to the topic):

HOOK (curiosity gap, ideally from something the viewer did today) → SETUP (only the context needed: what problem did they face?) → DEVELOPMENT (chronological or logical; each section answers one question and opens another) → ESCALATION (save the best for later) → TURNING POINT (the counter-intuitive part) → BIGGER PICTURE (what it meant for survival, society, technology…) → MODERN CONNECTION → ENDING (reconsider something ordinary).

Word budgets: 3 min ≈ 400–500 · 8 min ≈ 1,000–1,200 · 12 min ≈ 1,500–1,800 · 20 min ≈ 2,500–3,000. Don't pad.

Accuracy is non-negotiable: no invented discoveries, dates, scientists, quotes, measurements or statistics; hedge the uncertain; verify with web search when available.

## Visuals

- A recurring "guide" stickman from today (modern look) who visits the past, plus the historical characters (`caveman`, `king`, `knight` looks or your own).
- Chapter cards between sections (`TXT.title`) — they also become YouTube chapters.
- Maps and timelines (`GFX.timeline`, simple shapes), comparisons (`GFX.bars`), counters for numbers.
- Long videos: vary sets every 30–60 s, keep one visual motif (the fire, the river) returning.
- Render time grows with length (20 min ≈ 40–80 min on a laptop): render overnight or in the background.
